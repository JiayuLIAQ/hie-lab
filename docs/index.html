01 / Our focus

At the Healthy and Intelligent Environments (HiE) Lab, we connect built environment physics with human exposure. We combine experiments, environmental sensing, computational modelling and physics-assisted AI to develop and evaluate ventilation, cooling and pollution-control strategies.

Our research spans multiple scales, from people and rooms to buildings and their surroundings, with a focus on tropical conditions.

Healthy

Cleaner air, lower exposure and thermal conditions that support comfort and well-being.

Intelligent

Reliable sensing and predictive models that support better design and adaptive operation.

Resilient

Low-energy strategies to maintain comfort and healthy indoor environments during extreme heat, haze and pandemics.

02 / Research Theme

  1. 01

    Multiscale Airflow & Pollution Dynamics

    We investigate how unsteady airflow governs the movement, mixing, accumulation and removal of pollutants across the human breathing zone, rooms, buildings and their surroundings. Using particle image velocimetry (PIV), controlled experiments and computational fluid dynamics (CFD), we examine thermal plumes, turbulent transport and air exchange through building openings. This work connects fluid mechanisms across scales to explain pollutant pathways and inform ventilation and building design.

  2. 02

    Human Exposure & Pollution Control

    We study how pollutant sources, indoor–outdoor exchange and people's location and activities shape exposure to particles, bioaerosols and gases. Laboratory experiments and field measurements help us relate environmental concentrations to exposure in homes, workplaces, kitchens and transport cabins. We evaluate source control, ventilation, portable air cleaners and HVAC filtration, focusing on how pollutant type, device placement and operating conditions affect their ability to reduce exposure.

  3. 03

    Alternative Cooling & Climate, Haze and Pandemic Resilience

    We investigate low-energy cooling through fans, radiant systems and natural or mixed-mode ventilation. Experiments, field studies and modelling help us assess trade-offs among thermal comfort, humidity, air quality and energy use in tropical conditions. We also explore how cooling, ventilation and filtration can work together during extreme heat, haze and pandemics, adapting operation to changing outdoor pollution and infection risks while maintaining comfortable, healthy indoor environments.

  4. 04

    Trustworthy Sensing & Predictive Control

    We evaluate sensor accuracy and performance across operating conditions, and connect measurements with physical models to support reliable environmental prediction. Our developing work explores data fusion, reduced-order and multi-fidelity modelling, and physics-assisted AI, with attention to validation and uncertainty. The aim is predictive control of ventilation, cooling and air cleaning that makes effective use of limited measurements and adapts to changing occupancy, weather and outdoor air quality.
